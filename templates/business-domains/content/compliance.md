# Content — compliance + legal

Publishing is regulated at the intersection of copyright, speech, advertising, privacy, and accessibility. Most regulations are civil liability, not criminal — but civil losses + reputational damage are existential.

## Copyright

### DMCA (Digital Millennium Copyright Act, 17 USC 512)
US law creating safe harbor for online service providers; near-universal implementation pattern.

- **Safe harbor conditions**:
  - Designated DMCA agent registered with Copyright Office (online + $6 fee).
  - Designated agent contact info published (website + Copyright Office database).
  - Takedown procedure for infringement claims.
  - Counter-notice procedure for claimed non-infringement.
  - Repeat-infringer policy + termination.

- **Takedown notice requirements** (§ 512(c)(3)):
  - Identification of copyrighted work.
  - Identification of infringing material + location (URL).
  - Contact info.
  - Good-faith statement.
  - Under-penalty-of-perjury statement.
  - Physical or electronic signature.

- **Response timeline**:
  - "Expeditious" removal of infringing content (typically <24-72h).
  - Notify uploader.
  - Counter-notice window (10-14 business days).
  - If counter-notice + no lawsuit filed within 10-14 business days, restore content.

- **Penalties for false takedowns (§ 512(f))**: damages, costs, attorney fees.

### Other copyright considerations
- **Fair use** (US) — 4-factor test; subjective; best to err toward licensed content.
- **Creative Commons** — track license per asset; respect attribution + share-alike.
- **Public domain** — pre-1928 US works (shifting annually).
- **Orphan works** — unknown copyright holder; risky to use.
- **Trademark** — separate from copyright; affects brand names, logos.

### International copyright
- **Berne Convention** — automatic copyright in member countries.
- **EU Copyright Directive (2019) Article 17** — large platforms liable for user uploads; content filtering pressure.
- **UK CDPA** — 70 years post-author life default.
- **Moral rights** (France, Germany, Canada) — author's right to integrity + attribution, non-waivable in some jurisdictions.

## FTC Advertising Disclosure

### Sponsored content / native ads
- **FTC Endorsement Guides** (16 CFR Part 255) — clear + conspicuous disclosure required.
- "Sponsored" / "Paid partnership with X" / "Ad" labels required.
- Disclosure at the top of article (not buried at bottom).
- Same size/contrast as surrounding content.
- Applies to influencers, affiliates, paid links.

### Affiliate links
- Disclose affiliate relationships.
- FTC 2023 guidance tightens: specific endorsement = specific disclosure.

### Testimonials / reviews
- Must reflect typical experience.
- Disclose if compensated.

### Penalties
- FTC action ($50k+ per violation historically).
- State attorney general enforcement.

## Section 230 (Communications Decency Act, US)

- Immunity for platforms from liability for user-generated content (with exceptions: IP, federal criminal, sex trafficking FOSTA/SESTA).
- Foundation of comment sections + community content.
- Moderation does NOT waive immunity (clarification in 2023 case law).
- State-level erosion attempts (Texas HB 20, Florida SB 7072) — litigated.

## Privacy

### GDPR
- Email subscribers = personal data.
- Newsletter requires consent (opt-in basis).
- Comment form requires consent (+ IP stored).
- User accounts = personal data.
- Right to access, rectify, erase, portability.
- Data retention policy documented.

### CCPA / CPRA
- California consumers' rights.
- "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Info" link if third-party ads/tracking.
- Sensitive personal info categories.

### COPPA (Children's Online Privacy Protection Act)
- Applies to sites directed at children <13.
- Verifiable parental consent.
- Limited data collection.
- "General audience" sites targeting adults less strict.

### PIPEDA (Canada), LGPD (Brazil), POPIA (South Africa), PDPA (Singapore), PIPL (China)
- Similar consent-based models; applicable based on audience location.

### CAN-SPAM (US)
- Unsubscribe mechanism must work 10 days + process.
- Sender identification accurate.
- Subject line not deceptive.
- Physical mailing address in email.
- $50k+ per non-compliant email penalty ceiling.

### CASL (Canada Anti-Spam Law)
- Express consent required (stricter than CAN-SPAM).
- Unsubscribe within 10 days.
- Implied consent narrow (existing business relationship).
- Penalties up to $10M / organization.

### Email deliverability rules (Gmail / Yahoo 2024)
- Sender authentication: SPF + DKIM + DMARC required.
- List-Unsubscribe header + one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058).
- Spam complaint rate < 0.3% (preferred) / < 0.1% (target).
- From Gmail/Yahoo's perspective — not regulator — but deliverability-critical.

## Accessibility

- **WCAG 2.1 AA** — baseline standard.
- **WCAG 2.2** — current; additional criteria (focus visibility, dragging movements, target size).
- **ADA Title III (US)** — case law extends to commercial websites; lawsuits common against publishers.
- **Section 508** — federal procurement.
- **EN 301 549 (EU)** — public sector.
- **EAA (EU Accessibility Act 2025)** — private sector ecommerce + some digital services.
- **AODA (Ontario)** — publishers operating in Ontario.

Key requirements for publishers:
- Alt text on all meaningful images.
- Captions + transcripts for video.
- Audio transcripts.
- Keyboard navigation.
- Screen reader compatibility (ARIA).
- Color contrast ratios.
- Resize text without layout break.

## Defamation / libel

- **US — defamation law (state-level)**: false statement + publication + harm + fault.
- **Public figures** — "actual malice" standard (New York Times v. Sullivan).
- **Private figures** — negligence standard.
- **Statements of opinion** — protected; but dressing opinion as fact not.
- **UK defamation** — reversed burden of proof (defendant proves truth).
- **EU member states** — vary; some criminal defamation (Germany).

Editorial process:
- Fact-check protocol.
- Legal review for sensitive stories.
- Retraction + correction policy.
- Right-of-reply courtesy.

## Right of publicity

- **US (state law)** — misappropriation of name / likeness for commercial use.
- Vary by state; California + New York robust.
- Consent forms for people featured in stories.

## Privacy torts (US state-level)

- False light.
- Intrusion upon seclusion.
- Public disclosure of private facts.
- Misappropriation.

## Anti-bias laws (emerging)

- **NYC AEDT** — hiring algorithm audit (not directly for publishers but algorithmic recommendations relevant).
- **EU Digital Services Act (DSA)** — transparency reporting for large platforms; risk assessments for illegal content + disinformation.
- **EU Digital Markets Act (DMA)** — gatekeeper obligations; not most publishers.

## Political advertising

- **FEC (US)** — political ads disclosure (sponsored by).
- **State-level** — California, New York, Washington disclosure laws.
- **Meta / Google ad libraries** — self-regulated but audited.
- Publisher accepting political ads = additional disclosure + verification.

## Age-restricted content

- Tobacco, alcohol, firearms, gambling — age verification + geo-restriction.
- Adult content — 18 U.S.C. § 2257 record-keeping requirement.
- COPPA for content directed at children.

## Syndication + licensing

- **AP / Reuters / wire content** — licensed; may not copy-paste without agreement.
- **Attribution requirements** vary by license.
- **Embed policies** — Twitter, Instagram, TikTok embeds subject to their terms.
- **Canonical URLs** for syndicated content — SEO + legal.

## Data retention

- **User accounts** — per privacy policy; reasonable period post-inactivity.
- **Comment data** — retention policy; allow deletion on request.
- **Newsletter subscribers** — until unsubscribed + suppression list (retained for compliance — not sending, but proving no-send).
- **Analytics** — policy-defined; anonymize per 14 months common.
- **Archived content** — keep published content for historical record; corrections tracked.

## Advertising technology

- **GDPR consent for ads** — IAB TCF (Transparency + Consent Framework) 2.2.
- **CCPA opt-out** — GPC (Global Privacy Control) signal respected.
- **Cookie banner** — block non-essential until consent.
- **Third-party tracker disclosure** in privacy policy.

## Newsroom ethics (self-regulatory)

- **SPJ Code of Ethics** (US).
- **IFCN (International Fact-Checking Network)** principles.
- **Public Editor / Ombudsman** role.
- **Corrections policy** visible.
- **Anonymous sources** protocols.
- **Conflict of interest** disclosure.

## Common compliance gaps in v1

- No DMCA agent registered with Copyright Office; platform loses safe harbor.
- Takedown inbox at `copyright@` unmonitored; claims age; court orders.
- Counter-notice procedure absent.
- No repeat-infringer policy or enforcement; safe harbor waived.
- Image alt text not enforced; ADA + WCAG fail; lawsuit bait.
- Newsletter unsubscribe not one-click; Gmail bulk-sender rules fail.
- List-Unsubscribe header missing.
- Affiliate links without disclosure; FTC risk.
- Sponsored content labeled "Brand Studio" (not "Sponsored"); FTC ambiguity.
- Comments allow HTML including links without nofollow/ugc; SEO manipulation.
- User comment data exported doesn't honor GDPR right to access (only some fields).
- Subscriber consent capture without timestamp + IP + form version.
- Privacy policy copy-paste template; doesn't match actual practices.
- Cookies set before consent (violates GDPR + ePrivacy).
- Third-party embeds (YouTube, Twitter) set cookies before user consent.
- No archival of corrections — editors silently rewrite; loss of trust.
- Contributor agreement absent; work-for-hire ambiguity; copyright disputes.
- User-generated content moderation underinvested; Section 230 still protects but brand damage.
- Retention policy absent; subscribers from 2015 still on list.
- No data export for subscribers (GDPR right).
- Newsletter opt-in pre-checked (GDPR violation).
- No double opt-in (deliverability + consent issues).
- No SPF/DKIM/DMARC (deliverability + spoofing risk).
- Analytics stores full IP without anonymization (GDPR CCPA).
- No cookie consent management (international traffic violates).
- EAA (EU 2025) accessibility requirements not budgeted.
