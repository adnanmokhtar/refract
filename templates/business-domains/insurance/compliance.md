# Insurance — compliance + legal

Insurance is one of the most heavily regulated industries. Each US state has its own DOI; EU has Solvency II; markets emerging in MENA / APAC each have their own. Compliance is the product.

## Licensing

### US — state-by-state
- 50 different DOIs (Departments of Insurance) + DC + territories.
- Carrier license per state (admitted) OR surplus lines (non-admitted).
- Producer (agent / broker) license per state.
- Adjuster license per state for some lines.
- Resident vs non-resident license.
- Surplus lines: when admitted carriers won't write the risk; non-admitted carrier writes via surplus-lines broker; pays state surplus-lines tax.

### NAIC (National Association of Insurance Commissioners)
- Coordinates among states (model laws + system).
- SERFF for filings.
- Risk-based capital (RBC) standards.
- Model laws: Unfair Claims Settlement Practices Act, Producer Licensing Model Act, etc.

### EU — Solvency II
- EU-wide framework for insurer capital adequacy.
- Three pillars: quantitative requirements, governance, disclosure.
- ORSA (Own Risk and Solvency Assessment).
- Reporting to EIOPA + national supervisors.

### UK — FCA / PRA
- PRA prudential supervision.
- FCA conduct + consumer protection.
- Senior Managers and Certification Regime (SM&CR).

### Other
- Singapore: MAS.
- Australia: APRA.
- Japan: FSA.
- Saudi Arabia: SAMA.
- UAE: CBUAE / DFSA.
- India: IRDAI.

## Rate + form filings

- Insurers must file rates + forms with state DOI before using.
- Categories:
  - **Prior approval** — DOI must approve before use.
  - **File and use** — file then use after waiting period.
  - **Use and file** — file within X days of use.
  - **No file** — exempt (rare; commercial lines often).
- Form filings include: declarations, coverage forms, endorsements, exclusions.
- Rate filings include: rating tables, factor multipliers, classification systems.
- Filings tracked in SERFF (most states).
- Using unfiled forms / rates = regulatory violation.
- Each form filing has a version + effective date — code must reference version, not "current."

## Consumer protection

### Unfair Claims Settlement Practices Act (most US states)
- Acknowledge claims promptly (state-specific timing).
- Investigate promptly.
- Approve / deny within reasonable time.
- Pay approved claims promptly.
- Provide reasoning for denials.
- Cannot misrepresent policy provisions.
- Cannot delay to compel low settlement.
- Bad-faith litigation: insurer pays attorney fees + punitive damages if violated.

### State-specific timing examples
- California: 15 days to acknowledge; 40 days to approve/deny; 30 days to pay.
- Texas: 15 days to acknowledge.
- Florida: 14 days to acknowledge; 90 days for decision.

### Notice requirements
- Cancellation by insurer: 10-60 days advance (state-specific) by certified mail in many cases.
- Non-renewal: 30-60 days advance with reason.
- Policy change: at least at renewal; mid-term changes require endorsement.
- Premium increase >X%: enhanced disclosure in some states.

### Disclosure
- Schedule of premium + fees.
- Cancellation fees + short-rate vs pro-rata refund disclosure.
- Coverage limits + deductibles.
- Exclusions in clear language.

### Right to access (privacy)
- FCRA: when adverse decision based on credit/consumer report, must give adverse action notice + information about report.
- State privacy laws (NAIC Model Privacy Act / Reg).

### Health insurance specific
- Mental Health Parity Act (MHPAEA).
- ACA coverage requirements (essential health benefits, pre-existing condition exclusion banned).
- HIPAA portability / privacy.
- Medicare / Medicaid rules (CMS).

## Privacy

### Insurance-specific
- NAIC Insurance Information and Privacy Protection Act / state regs.
- Notice at application + annually.
- Opt-out for non-affiliated sharing.

### GLBA (US)
- Applies to insurance.
- Privacy notice + safeguards rule.

### HIPAA
- Health + life insurance touching PHI subject to HIPAA.
- BAA with vendors.
- Breach notification.

### GDPR
- EU policyholder data.
- Lawful basis: contract (policy), legitimate interest (fraud), consent (marketing).
- Special category: health data needs explicit consent OR vital-interest basis.
- Right of access modulated by claim-investigation needs.

### CCPA / CPRA
- California; insurance partially covered.

### State data breach notification
- All 50 states + DC + territories have laws.
- Timing varies (30-90 days typical).

### Cybersecurity
- NYDFS Part 500 (NY-licensed) — extensive requirements: CISO, MFA, encryption, incident reporting in 72h.
- Other states adopting similar.
- Insurance Data Security Model Law (NAIC) — state adoption growing.

## Anti-fraud

- Most US states require insurers to operate Special Investigations Unit (SIU).
- Mandatory reporting of suspected fraud to state insurance fraud bureau.
- Anti-fraud warnings on applications + claims (state-specific text required).
- "Insurance fraud is a crime" disclosures.
- Coalition Against Insurance Fraud (industry group).
- ISO ClaimSearch — industry-wide fraud database.

## Producer + market conduct

### Producer licensing
- License + appointment per state.
- Continuing education requirements.
- Fingerprinting + background.
- Anti-money-laundering training (life lines).
- E&O insurance for producers.

### Market conduct exams
- State DOIs periodically examine insurers' practices.
- Sample claims, applications, cancellations.
- Findings → orders → fines.

### Replacement rules (life/health)
- When replacing existing policy, specific disclosures + waiting periods required.
- Suitability (annuities) — products must be suitable for the buyer.

## Tax

### Premium tax
- US: state-by-state premium tax (1-4% typically) on direct written premium.
- Surplus lines: premium tax + stamping fees.
- Self-procurement tax.

### Income tax (insurer)
- Premium income.
- Investment income.
- Loss reserves (deductible per IRC).
- Special insurer accounting (SAP — Statutory Accounting Principles, vs GAAP).

### Customer-side tax
- Premiums generally not deductible for individuals (HSA-related exceptions).
- Business: premiums often deductible.
- Life insurance death benefits generally tax-free.
- Reporting: 1099-MISC for some payouts; 1099-LTC; 1099-R for annuity withdrawals.

### EU
- IPT (Insurance Premium Tax) varies by country (1-23%).

## Solvency / financial reporting

### US
- Annual + Quarterly Statement (the "blank") to state DOI + NAIC.
- Schedule P (loss reserves).
- Audited financial statements.
- RBC reporting.
- ORSA (large insurers).
- Holding company filings (Form B/C/F).

### EU
- Solvency II quarterly + annual reporting.
- ORSA.
- SFCR (public report).

## Reinsurance

- Treaties + facultative.
- Cession reporting.
- Recoverable accounting.
- Collateral requirements (US for non-US reinsurers).
- Captive insurance compliance.

## Claim-specific compliance

- State-required denial letter content + format.
- Statute of limitations on claims (state + line specific, 1-6 years from loss typically).
- Subrogation rules (some states make-whole rule, some not).
- Comparative negligence rules (state-specific) affect partial liability.

## Marketing + advertising

- State-specific advertising rules + filings.
- Misleading / unfair advertising prohibited.
- Testimonials + endorsements regulated.
- Comparison advertising (often prohibited or restricted).

## Insurance fraud (criminal)

- Federal mail fraud / wire fraud apply.
- State insurance fraud statutes.
- Mandatory reporting per state.
- ISO ClaimSearch contributions.

## Records retention

| Record | Retention | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Policies (full lifecycle + 5-10 years post-expiry) | Often 5-7 years post-expiry | Litigation + statute of limitations |
| Claims | 5-10 years post-close (longer for liability claims) | SOL + reopening + bad-faith |
| Underwriting decisions | 5-7 years | Examiner review |
| Cancellation/denial letters | 5+ years | Bad-faith defense |
| Producer records | 5-7 years post-termination | Examiner |
| Financial / tax | 7+ years | IRS + state revenue |
| HIPAA-related | 6 years from creation | HIPAA |
| Audit logs | 5-7 years | Reg + bad-faith |

## Insurance-specific compliance gaps in v1

- Generic decline letter (no state-required language) → bad-faith defense weakened.
- Cancellation by email when state requires certified mail → cancellation invalid + claim must be honored.
- Coverage at loss time computed from CURRENT policy state, not historical — endorsement-related disputes lost.
- Adverse action notice missing on credit-score declines → FCRA violation + class action.
- Beneficiary change online with no verification → fraud + family lawsuits.
- "Replacement" of existing life policy without disclosure → state suitability violation.
- ACA pre-existing condition exclusion in health → federal violation.
- Anti-fraud warnings missing on claims forms → forms invalid.
- SIU referral process absent → state DOI fines.
- Producer commission paid before licensing verified → unauthorized practice.
- Claim filed online without ability to upload during outage → state claim-handling timing missed.
- Denial reason not policy-cited → bad-faith litigation.
- Document retention purged at company-set 1 year → SOL extends 5+ years; lost evidence.
- Form using unfiled rate / form → state DOI fines + invalid policies.
- Multi-state operation with single set of forms → state-specific provisions missing.
- Catastrophe (hurricane) FNOL surge causes outage → DOI complaints + market conduct fines.
- Privacy notice not given annually → GLBA violation.
